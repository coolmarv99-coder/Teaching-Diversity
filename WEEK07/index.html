Opening vignette

“Why Does Everything Have to Become a Conflict?”

Derek: “Sometimes I feel like people are just looking for things to be offended by. Can’t teachers just be respectful and move on?”

Jordan: “But what happens when a family’s expectations are different from the program’s routines?”

Aaliyah: “That’s where conflict can happen—food, discipline, holidays, clothing, gender roles, communication, or who should be involved in decisions.”

Maya: “And if teachers assume their way is the normal way, they might see the family as difficult instead of trying to understand the difference.”

Derek: “But what if the teacher really is just trying to follow the rules?”

Aaliyah: “Resolving conflict is not only about proving who is right. It’s about listening carefully, respecting families, explaining program expectations clearly, and looking for solutions that support the child.”

Maya: “The goal is not to avoid every disagreement. The goal is to handle disagreements in a way that protects relationships.”

Think first: How can educators respond when cultural differences lead to conflict without becoming defensive, blaming families, or ignoring program responsibilities?
