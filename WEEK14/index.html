I Didn’t Think That Was Me

Before class begins, Maya, Jordan, and Aaliyah are talking about the week’s reading on personal bias.

Jordan is quieter than usual. Finally, he says, “I did something weird last night. I took one of those online implicit bias tests. I thought it would show that I’m pretty fair-minded.”

Maya looks over. “And?”

Jordan shrugs. “The result bothered me. I don’t want to overreact to one test, but it made me wonder if I carry assumptions I don’t even notice.”

Aaliyah nods. “That’s uncomfortable, but maybe that’s the point. Bias is not always something we choose on purpose. Sometimes it comes from messages we’ve heard for years — from family, school, media, friends, or the larger culture.”

Jordan looks embarrassed. “So does that mean I’m secretly prejudiced?”

“I don’t think that’s the most helpful question,” Maya says. “Maybe the better question is what you do once you notice a possible bias.”

Aaliyah adds, “Right. Being equity-minded does not mean pretending we have no bias. It means being willing to reflect, slow down, and question our first reactions before they affect children and families.”

Jordan nods slowly. “So the goal is not to prove I’m bias-free. The goal is to become more aware and more responsible.”

Maya looks back at the chapter title. “Maybe personal bias is not just something to feel guilty about. It is something to examine, so we can become better teachers.”
