Opening vignette

Who Counts as Family?

Before class begins, Maya, Jordan, and Aaliyah are talking about the week’s reading on diverse family structures.

Jordan says, “I know families can look different, but sometimes I still catch myself picturing a mom, a dad, and their children when I hear the word family.”

Aaliyah nods. “A lot of people do. But children may live with one parent, two parents, grandparents, foster parents, adoptive parents, extended relatives, same-sex parents, blended families, or other caregivers who are important in their lives.”

Maya thinks about classroom activities. “So when teachers ask children to make a Mother’s Day card, draw their family, or bring a parent to school, they may not realize those activities can feel complicated for some children.”

Jordan pauses. “I guess even simple words like ‘mom and dad’ can leave some families out.”

“Exactly,” Aaliyah says. “That doesn’t mean teachers can never talk about mothers or fathers. It means we should use language and activities that make room for many kinds of families.”

Maya looks back at the chapter title. “Maybe the question is not whether a family matches what we expected. The question is whether the child knows their family belongs in the classroom community.”

Think first: How can early childhood educators use language, materials, and activities that respect and include many different family structures?
