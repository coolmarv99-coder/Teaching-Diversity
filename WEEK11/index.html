More Than a Diagnosis

Before class begins, Maya, Jordan, and Aaliyah are talking about the week’s reading on families of children with special needs or special health care needs.

Jordan says, “I know inclusion is important, but I wonder if teachers sometimes feel unprepared. What if a child has needs the teacher does not fully understand?”

Aaliyah is quiet for a moment. “My younger brother has a speech delay and some sensory needs. When he was little, some adults were patient with him, but others acted like his diagnosis explained everything about him.”

Maya listens carefully. “That must have been hard for your family.”

“It was,” Aaliyah says. “My family did not expect teachers to know everything right away. But we could tell the difference between teachers who saw him as a problem and teachers who wanted to understand him as a child.”

Jordan nods. “So the diagnosis might give teachers some information, but it should not become the whole story.”

“Exactly,” Aaliyah says. “Teachers need to ask questions, listen to families, work with specialists when needed, and think about what support helps the child participate.”

Maya looks back at the chapter title. “That sounds like equity again. Not every child needs the same support, but every child deserves real belonging.”

Aaliyah smiles. “Yes. Inclusion is not just placing a child in the room. It means helping the child become a full member of the classroom community.”

Think first: How can early childhood educators support children with special needs or special health care needs while seeing each child as more than a diagnosis or label?
