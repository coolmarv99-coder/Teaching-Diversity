Opening vignette

“What Do We Notice, and What Do We Assume?”

Jordan: “Race, ethnicity, culture, language—sometimes I’m not sure I understand the differences.”

Aaliyah: “Those words are related, but they don’t all mean the same thing. We can’t assume we understand a child’s family, culture, or language just by looking at them.”

Maya: “If a teacher assumes what language a family speaks, where they are from, or what traditions they follow, the teacher might miss who that family really is.”

Jordan: “And if a child speaks one language at home and another at school, that’s not a problem to fix. It’s part of who they are.”

Aaliyah: “Supporting home language can help children feel connected to family, culture, and identity. It also shows families that their knowledge and experiences matter.”

Maya: “Maybe the goal is not to pretend we don’t notice race, ethnicity, or language. The goal is to notice respectfully, ask carefully, and avoid making assumptions.”

Think first: How can educators respect children’s race, ethnicity, culture, and home language without making assumptions about children or families?
